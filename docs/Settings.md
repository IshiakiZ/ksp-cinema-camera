# Settings

Open the Keystone window (the keystone button on the game's toolbar, or the modifier key and K:
Option-K on a Mac, Alt-K elsewhere), turn to **Cinema Camera** and switch it on. Every number can be
dragged to or typed into the box beside its slider.

| Setting | Installed as | What it does to the picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cinema camera | off | on: the flight camera takes its angle of view from the lens, and the rest below applies. Off: the game's camera exactly as it was, at the zoom it had |
| Focal length | 50 mm | how much the camera sees. 12 mm is very wide, 300 mm a telescope (4.6 degrees). The game's own zoom keys turn this ring too |
| Sensor | full frame | 36 x 24 mm, Super 35 (24.9 x 18.7) or IMAX 70 mm (70 x 52.6). The same lens sees wider on a bigger sensor and blurs the background more |
| Aperture | f/2.8 | the f-number, 1 to 22. Small numbers: a thin slice in focus and a soft background. Large: nearly everything sharp |
| Focus on | the ship | the ship being flown (the part of it you are looking at, if you are looking at it), whatever is in the middle of the picture, or a distance set by hand |
| Distance | 10 m | where the focus is when set by hand |
| Focus speed | 4 | how quickly the focus follows. It moves as a lens ring does: as long from 2 m to 4 m as from 100 m to infinity |
| Blur quality | medium | how large the out-of-focus blur may get; larger takes more of the graphics card's time |
| Shutter | 1/120 s | how long the shutter is open: motion blur. It cannot be open longer than one frame of the game lasts (at 60 frames a second, 1/60 s) |
| Film speed | ISO 100 | fast film is grainy, and takes less light |
| Exposure | left alone | `by the camera`: how bright the picture comes out goes by the aperture, the shutter and the film speed. Full sun wants f/16 at 1/100 s on ISO 100 film, or anything that lets in as much (the photographer's "sunny sixteen"); every stop the lens is opened, or halving of the shutter's speed, or doubling of the film's, is twice the light. Needs the TUFX mod. `left alone`: as bright as the game, or another mod's eye (Natural Light's), makes it |
| Grey filter | 0 stops | a grey filter in front of the lens, holding the light back by so many stops: for a wide-open lens or a slow shutter in daylight |
| Lens character | 0.3 | darker corners (most with the aperture wide open), a little colour fringing, and the bend of a lens shorter than 35 mm. None at 0 |
| Bars | none | black bars for a 1.85:1 or 2.39:1 picture, drawn only while the game's interface is hidden (F2) or the viewfinder is up |
| Camera move | none | `stay here and follow`: the camera is left where it is, fixed to the ground (or to the spot above the ground that it is at), and turns to follow the ship as it flies off, a moment behind, as a hand on a tripod does. `circle the ship`: the camera goes slowly round it. `drift and follow`: for space; the camera is left where it is, going on at the speed the ship had, as a camera on a second spacecraft would, so that a burn takes the ship away from it. `fly the path`: the camera flies the path set for the craft. A move is never kept from one flight to the next. See [Moves and paths](Moves-and-paths.md) |
| Zoom to keep the ship's size | on | while the camera stays behind, the lens is drawn out as the ship gets further off, so that it stays the size it was in the picture when the camera was left: as far as 2400 mm, which is what the cameras that follow real launches have. Set the focal length first: that is the size it keeps |
| Circling | 6° a second | how fast the camera goes round the ship; less than nought, the other way |
| Path takes | 10 s | how long the camera takes to fly the path, from its first point to its last |
| Viewfinder | off | `on`: the game's own interface is put away and a camera's is drawn in its place. `with thirds` adds the lines a picture is composed on. See [The viewfinder](The-viewfinder.md) |
| Meter | centre-weighted | the viewfinder's light meter: how far over or under a middle grey the picture comes out, in stops. `centre-weighted`: the whole picture, its middle counting most. `spot`: only what is inside the focus bracket. `off`: no meter |

The page also reads out what the markings on a real lens would tell you: the angle of view, where the
focus is, and from where to where the picture is sharp (to a blur of 0.03 mm on full-frame film, the usual
measure), for example `85 mm on full frame: 16.1° from top to bottom. In focus at 45.0 m; sharp from
35.7 m to 60.9 m.`

## Keys

All with the game's modifier key held (Option on a Mac, Alt elsewhere), in flight:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `C` | the camera on and off |
| `V` | the viewfinder up and down |
| `F` | what the focus is on: the ship, the middle, by hand |
| `T` | leave the camera where it is, to follow the ship from there (fixed to the ground near a world, drifting in space); again, to take it back to the ship |
| `Y` | fly the path; again, to stop it |
| `Page Up`, `Page Down` | pull the focus further and nearer by hand, for as long as they are held (taking hold of the ring from wherever the focus was) |

The game reads its own keys whether the modifier is held or not, and its own `T`, `F`, `V` and `C` would
switch SAS on or off, hold it, change the game's camera mode and show or hide the crew's portraits. While
the camera is running and the modifier is held, those four of the game's are held off. (With the camera
switched off, the modifier and `C` still also show or hide the crew's portraits as it switches the camera
on.)

## The path

Under **Path**, at the foot of the page: **Put a point here** (the camera's place, which way it looks and
its lens, as they are now), **Take the last away**, **Clear**, and **Fly it**. A path is kept for the craft
being flown, by its name. See [Moves and paths](Moves-and-paths.md).
